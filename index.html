SOC Guide for Operational Excellence

A unified operational reference for Microsoft Defender XDR — MDO, MDE, MDI, and MDA — organized around the routines analysts use to triage, tune, hunt, validate coverage, and respond across Defender XDR.

What this is

The day-to-day playbook for running Defender XDR. Each task lists the portal you'd open, the role you'd need, and the output it should leave behind.

Why cadence matters

SOC work is easier to execute when routine tasks follow a clear rhythm: daily triage, weekly tuning, and periodic posture review. Structuring the work this way reveals coverage gaps and makes handoffs explicit.

Why patterns matter

Six patterns repeat across all four workloads — Triage, Tune, Hunt, Intel, Detect, Config. Recognizing them speeds onboarding and frames cross-workload incidents.

Why this matters

Triage with less friction

Daily routines help analysts identify what is urgent, who owns it, what evidence matters, and what action comes next.

Reduce repeat noise

False-positive review, false-negative review, alert classification, tuning, and Microsoft submissions help keep recurring low-value alerts from returning to the queue.

Protect investigation visibility

Sensor health, connector status, exclusions, and policy posture determine whether analysts can trust the data in front of them. Routine checks reduce the risk of investigating with missing or stale telemetry.

Connect signals across workloads

A phish, endpoint alert, identity anomaly, and SaaS event may be part of the same attack. Shared SOC patterns help analysts connect related activity instead of treating each alert in isolation.
